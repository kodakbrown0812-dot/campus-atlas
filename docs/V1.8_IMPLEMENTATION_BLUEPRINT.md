# Campus Atlas V1.8 — Implementation Blueprint

**Status:** Proposed frozen implementation authority for V1.8

**Blueprint branch:** `v1.8-blueprint`

**Repository baseline audited:** `main` at `66990cb00e2fe460ff9a63ccc22a51034e6de7c7`

**Scope:** Research, evaluation, retrieval intelligence, company benchmark, scaling, and thin operational evaluation UX

**Out of scope for this document:** Production implementation, schema migration, deployment, canonical-data mutation, Mock Company construction, embeddings, and provider dispatch

## Governing doctrine

V1.8 is governed by these statements:

- The transformer performs fresh computation.
- Atlas preserves governed learning outside the model.
- The context packet is the primary product.
- Content learning changes durable understanding and requires evidence, scope, versioning, and governance.
- Retrieval learning changes how governed knowledge is found and reconstructed, but must not silently rewrite truth.
- Receipts explain what changed, what was supplied, what was excluded, and why.
- Company-first is the commercial thesis.
- Cody and Campus projects are high-frequency adversarial dogfood, not proof of the final market.
- New infrastructure must be justified by measured failures.
- The central benchmark is downstream causal value, not retrieval aesthetics.

The canonical V1.8 proof question is:

> Did Atlas provide a smaller, better-governed packet that caused the receiving model to avoid an important error made by transcript windows, keyword retrieval, vector-only RAG, or V1.7?

The canonical Atlas claim is:

> Atlas does not make the transformer permanently remember. Atlas makes every fresh transformer computation begin from the best governed reconstruction of what has been learned so far, while preserving an inspectable record of how that reconstruction was produced.

## Evidence key

This blueprint uses four evidence labels:

- **Proven:** implemented and validated by repository tests or the preserved local dogfood record.
- **Partially proven:** a narrower implementation exists, but the proposed V1.8 claim has not been validated.
- **Hypothesis:** requires a prospective V1.8 experiment.
- **Deferred:** deliberately outside the current acceptance boundary.

The repository and its canonical records are factual implementation authority. Product language in this blueprint does not upgrade a hypothesis into a fact.

### V1.8 hypothesis registry

| ID | Prospective hypothesis | Primary falsification evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | Governed Atlas packets reduce consequential continuity errors versus transcript, summary, keyword, and vector-only baselines | Paired downstream answers show no material error reduction or show worse contamination |
| H2 | Need Gate plus route-before-rank keeps context selective without losing consequential dependencies | False-negative continuity dependencies or packet size/scans grow with corpus without bounded benefit |
| H3 | Scope, correction, conflict, and lineage governance create value beyond retrieval relevance alone | Baselines with equal retrieved content perform equivalently and Atlas governance adds no downstream protection |
| H4 | Company continuity traps reproduce the value seen in Cody dogfood | Mock Company results do not generalize across departments/tasks or depend on sports-specific mechanism families |
| H5 | Measured lexical and bounded graph improvements can expose whether embeddings are necessary | Residual semantic recall failures remain consequential after lexical/graph work, opening the embedding gate |

### V1.8 architectural risk register

| ID | Risk | Required control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Benchmark leakage or Atlas receiving privileged truth | Hidden-label isolation, identical current-state inputs, and per-system input receipts |
| R2 | Retrieval optimization becomes hidden semantic authority | Separate retrieval configuration, immutable canonical meaning, and hard authority invariants |
| R3 | Synthetic overfitting creates a flattering company story | Frozen holdouts, ablations, cross-domain fixtures, and later external pilots |
| R4 | Broad project-family scans become noisy and expensive | True staged telemetry, route-first filtering, scaling tiers, and explicit infrastructure gates |
| R5 | Deployment/source divergence invalidates evidence | Complete Issue Zero before treating hosted runs as benchmark or release evidence |

## 1. V1.8 problem statement

### 1.1 What V1.7 proved

V1.7 locally proved a causal continuity loop through immutable packet compilation:

`Exact source → canonical events → selected reasoning nodes → atomic finding → Cody governance → scoped mechanism → later task → roadway and treatment → bounded immutable packet → causal receipt`

Evidence:

- The formal audit accepted the local vision-board loop with 28 PASS, 1 FAIL, 6 DEFERRED, and 2 NOT TESTED results: `docs/V1.7_FORMAL_AUDIT.md`.
- The preserved Sports Engine proof is recorded in `docs/V1.7_NEXT_CHAT_TRANSFER.md` and the local canonical D1 state.
- The immutable dogfood packet is `packet:ab0bb475-2713-43ab-9f65-fea0f4281c40`, compiled at 278 estimated tokens under an 800-token budget, with one approved-local mechanism, five required checks, five audit-only lineage ancestors, and no handoff.
- `worker/packet-service.ts`, `worker/packet-eligibility.ts`, and the Slice 4–6 tests protect bounded compilation, lineage-only exclusion, protected material, and immutable reads.
- `worker/governance-service.ts` protects reviewed wording, scope, append-only authority transitions, rejection, deferral, and rollback.
- PR #21 reconciled the native dogfood evidence/runbook with the completed local packet proof; PR #20 merged the formal V1.7 audit and next-chat transfer. Neither converts deferred hosted or provider gates into passes.

V1.7.1 then proved additive API-first groundwork:

- `POST /api/v1/projects/:projectId/continuity/check` provides a zero-mutation `none | light | full` Need Gate through `worker/continuity-check-service.ts`.
- `POST /api/v1/projects/:projectId/reconstruction/run` performs replay-before-preflight idempotency, current preflight for new runs, zero-write stopped results, and canonical compilation through `worker/reconstruction-run-service.ts`.
- Both reuse existing interpretation, treatment, packet, and receipt ownership rather than creating a second engine. See `docs/V1.7.1_SLICE_A_CONTINUITY_CHECK.md`, `docs/V1.7.1_SLICE_B_RECONSTRUCTION_RUN.md`, PR #18, and PR #19.

### 1.2 What V1.8 must prove

V1.8 must move from “the governed engine can work” to “the governed engine produces measurable downstream value under realistic company continuity pressure.” It must prove or falsify five separate propositions:

1. **Evaluation validity:** Atlas behavior can be scored prospectively, deterministically, and reproducibly without relying on retrospective storytelling.
2. **Retrieval value:** Atlas finds the consequential governed context while excluding plausible but wrong-scope, stale, redundant, or non-authoritative material.
3. **Downstream causal value:** A receiving model using an Atlas packet avoids important errors that comparison systems make on the same task and evidence.
4. **Scale selectivity:** Relevant model-visible context stays bounded as durable history grows.
5. **Company usefulness:** The value survives outside Cody’s sports and product-development dogfood in a multi-department company benchmark.

### 1.3 Claims must remain separated

| Claim | Current status | V1.8 burden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local engine validity | **Proven** for the accepted Proto One loop | Preserve all V1.7 invariants while evaluating it |
| Production readiness | **Not proven** | Reconcile deployed source, then retain explicit hosted gates |
| Company usefulness | **Hypothesis** | Demonstrate on Mock Company and later external pilots |
| Retrieval scale | **Not tested** beyond the current small corpus | Measure staged retrieval at 100, 1,000, 10,000, and multi-year histories |
| Downstream reasoning advantage | **Not tested** | Compare prospectively scored receiving-model answers across baselines |
| Product-market fit | **Unvalidated** | Requires external user behavior and willingness-to-adopt evidence after V1.8 |

V1.8 succeeds only if it produces falsifiable evidence. More sophisticated retrieval that does not improve consequential downstream results is not a successful outcome.

If a public artifact cannot name a verifiable repository commit and build identity:

- screenshots can be attributed to the wrong implementation;
- hosted behavior can be mistaken for locally validated canonical behavior;
- regression claims cannot be tied to a reproducible artifact;
- stale seeded or V4.6 UI can be mistaken for current Atlas state;
- benchmark outcomes cannot be audited against source.

This is not an invitation to redesign the UI. It is a release-evidence repair.

### 3.3 Slice 0 acceptance criteria

Deployment/source reconciliation passes only when:

1. The currently public artifact is identified as canonical, stale, or noncanonical.
2. The repository commit and exact build command for any canonical deployment are recorded.
3. The deployed artifact exposes, through a non-secret health/build endpoint or advanced status view:
   - repository commit SHA;
   - build identifier and timestamp;
   - application/API version;
   - schema migration level;
   - environment identity such as local, preview, or production;
   - canonical persistence mode;
   - fixture/test-mode state.
4. The exposed identity is generated from the deployed build, not typed manually into a page.
5. The deployment process refuses or visibly labels an unknown/uncommitted source identity.
6. The public URL is checked against the expected identity after deployment.
7. No source reconciliation step mutates canonical dogfood data.
8. No stale public UI is described as the current canonical engine.

The rollback boundary is deployment metadata and the build-identity exposure only. No product feature work belongs in Issue Zero.

## 4. Content learning versus retrieval learning

### 4.1 Content learning

Content learning changes durable meaning. Examples include:

- a new or revised finding;
- an approved local or project mechanism;
- a correction to reasoning wording;
- a narrowed or widened governed scope;
- a challenge or unresolved conflict;
- a supersession or retirement;
- an outcome and reconciled postmortem;
- an approved policy or decision.

Content learning must be:

- grounded in canonical source or explicitly labeled representation;
- versioned and immutable in history;
- scoped;
- attributed to an actor;
- governed before it changes consequential retrieval authority;
- inspectable through lineage and receipts.

Existing ownership is already strong: `worker/checkpoint-service.ts`, `worker/governance-service.ts`, `worker/stewardship-mutations.ts`, the canonical schema in `db/schema.ts`, and migrations `0000`–`0008`.

### 4.2 Retrieval learning

Retrieval learning changes how existing governed knowledge is discovered and reconstructed. Examples include:

- Need Gate calibration;
- roadway selection features;
- eligible source-family selection;
- lexical matching;
- graph traversal order and hop bounds;
- semantic candidate discovery;
- reranking weights;
- widening and stopping rules;
- compact indexes or projections;
- redundancy collapse;
- source-expansion policy.

Retrieval learning may be experimentally calibrated, but it must not change:

- a record’s content;
- representation;
- authority;
- project/case scope;
- governance status;
- conflict resolution;
- truth state;
- canonical version history.

### 4.3 Required separation

Every retrieval experiment must operate through a versioned, reversible retrieval configuration whose identity is recorded with the evaluation run. It may propose a later content correction, but it cannot apply one.

Minimum safeguards:

1. Retrieval configurations are not canonical semantic records.
2. Candidate discovery scores cannot grant `Use` or authority.
3. An embedding or graph edge cannot transfer authority across project or case scope.
4. Repeated model retrieval does not validate the model’s own inference.
5. A retrieval change is evaluated against frozen expected labels before adoption.
6. Rollback restores the prior retrieval configuration without rewriting packets produced under either version.
7. Receipts name the retrieval configuration and which canonical rules ultimately controlled treatment.

This boundary prevents retrieval optimization from becoming hidden semantic learning.

## 5. Deterministic evaluation architecture

### 5.1 Objective

The first major V1.8 system is a provider-free evaluation harness that records expected behavior before execution, runs one or more retrieval systems against isolated canonical fixtures, and reports invariant violations and calibration differences without mutating production data.

The harness should be a test/evaluation package, not another canonical application authority. It should initially reuse the current Node test stack and isolated SQLite-backed D1 adapter from `tests/rendered-html.test.mjs`.

### 5.2 Prospective fixture contract

Each fixture should contain conceptually equivalent fields to:

```ts
type AtlasEvaluationFixture = {
  fixtureVersion: string;
  fixtureId: string;
  title: string;
  domain: string;
  corpusSnapshotId: string;
  literalTask: string;
  requestedOutput?: string;
  projectId: string;
  caseId?: string;
  runtimeInputs: {
    roadwayOverride?: string;
    tokenBudget: 400 | 800 | 1600;
    reviewPolicy: "automatic" | "required" | "clarify";
  };
  expected: {
    needGate: "none" | "light" | "full";
    roadwayId?: string;
    use: ExpectedCandidate[];
    consider: ExpectedCandidate[];
    exclude: ExpectedCandidate[];
    protectedCorrections: string[];
    protectedConflicts: string[];
    protectedChallenges: string[];
    missingLiveState: string[];
    packetBounds?: {
      maximumTokens: 400 | 800 | 1600;
      requiredSourceIds: string[];
      prohibitedSourceIds: string[];
    };
    stoppingReason: string;
    downstreamAnswerCriteria: AnswerCriterion[];
    prohibitedAnswerClaims: string[];
  };
  rationale: string;
  expectedLabelsAuthoredAt: string;
  expectedLabelsAuthoredBy: string;
};
```

The implementation may refine names, but it must preserve prospective expected treatment, packet boundaries, stopping behavior, and downstream criteria.

### 5.3 Fixture integrity

- Expected labels are frozen before the evaluated run.
- Fixture changes create a new fixture version.
- Corpus truth, expected retrieval, and expected downstream answer are stored separately.
- A system under test cannot read hidden expected labels.
- The runner records source commit, schema level, retrieval configuration, seed, and clock.
- Fixtures execute against isolated databases; live dogfood D1 is never an evaluation target.
- Failures produce durable evaluation artifacts only in the evaluation environment, not canonical product records.

### 5.4 Hard invariants

These are release-blocking and are not averaged away:

| Invariant | Required value |
| --- | --- |
| Cross-project leakage | 0 |
| Wrong-case governing leakage | 0 |
| Unsupported authority promotions | 0 |
| Historical packet mutations | 0 |
| Applicable protected correction/conflict loss | 0 |
| Test adapter represented as production success | 0 |
| Client-authored canonical treatment accepted | 0 |
| Provider calls in deterministic evaluation | 0 |

### 5.5 Calibration metrics

These are measured before thresholds are selected:

- Need Gate false positives and false negatives;
- primary-roadway accuracy and ambiguity accuracy;
- retrieval precision and recall against expected consequential candidates;
- `Consider` precision;
- stale-state inclusion rate;
- strongest-challenge retention;
- protected-correction/conflict retention;
- token utilization and irrelevant-token share;
- Exact-source expansion count;
- reconstruction determinism;
- end-to-end and per-stage latency;
- records scanned and surviving each gate;
- widening count;
- stopping-reason accuracy;
- downstream consequential-error rate by error class.

Thresholds must be calibrated from the first benchmark corpus. This blueprint intentionally does not invent target percentages.

### 5.6 Evaluation result artifact

Each result should record:

- fixture and corpus version;
- retrieval-system identity and configuration;
- actual Need Gate, roadway, treatment, packet, and receipt projection;
- invariant results;
- calibration metrics;
- deterministic receiver result;
- downstream score and grader identity;
- exact diff from expected behavior;
- execution timing and cost;
- source commit;
- pass, fail, or not-applicable status;
- no semantic authority.

## 6. Deterministic receiver and replay design

### 6.1 Purpose

V1.8 needs a provider-free receiver boundary that proves exactly what an eventual receiving model would receive and supports repeatable downstream scoring. It must not pretend to be a production model.

The existing `worker/receiving-model.ts` already proves an important transport boundary:

- the original request and Atlas context are separate content roles;
- the packet is hashed;
- a test-only adapter is explicitly labeled;
- production uses the OpenAI Responses API only with configuration;
- missing configuration fails honestly.

This is **partially proven**, not the V1.8 evaluation receiver.

### 6.2 Required receiver contract

The deterministic receiver should accept:

- exact original request and its hash;
- exact immutable packet snapshot and packet hash;
- packet ID/version;
- role/block map showing user task versus Atlas-supplied context;
- deterministic receiver program/version;
- optional fixture-specific scoring hooks;
- replay ID and prior result reference.

It should emit:

- received hashes and byte counts;
- verified role separation;
- deterministic structured answer or rule-evaluation result;
- criteria satisfied/violated;
- error-class labels;
- prohibited-claim detection;
- replay equivalence;
- explicit `testOnly: true` and `providerCallPerformed: false`.

### 6.3 Replay guarantees

- Replay uses the exact saved packet; it never recompiles.
- The same receiver version, fixture, task, and packet produces the same result.
- Historical packet and receipt remain immutable.
- A receiver-version change creates a new evaluation result.
- Results may reference canonical packets but cannot change canonical authority.

### 6.4 What it proves

- task/packet separation;
- byte-accurate transport;
- packet immutability;
- deterministic replay;
- scoring-pipeline correctness;
- whether a packet contains or omits the facts needed for predefined answer criteria;
- no paid-provider dependency for routine regression testing.

### 6.5 What it does not prove

- that a frontier model will follow every packet instruction;
- model quality or real-world correctness;
- provider latency, uptime, or pricing;
- tool-use behavior;
- production credential handling;
- causal benefit across nondeterministic model samples;
- customer value.

One or more later, budgeted real-provider experiments remain necessary for the production provider boundary, but they are not required for deterministic V1.8 development.

## 7. Retrieval telemetry

### 7.1 Per-run telemetry contract

Every evaluated retrieval run should report:

| Stage | Required telemetry |
| --- | --- |
| Need Gate | level, reason, metadata records inspected, latency |
| Project boundary | records scanned, records surviving, rejected cross-project IDs/count |
| Case boundary | records entering, records surviving, local-scope exclusions |
| Source-family routing | candidate families eligible/searched/skipped and reason |
| Authority/status/freshness | counts before and after each gate |
| Discovery | lexical/entity/semantic/relationship hits by source family |
| Graph | nodes visited, edges traversed by type, hop depth, stopped branches |
| Reranking | candidates reranked, score components, rank changes |
| Exact expansion | Exact sources opened, bytes/tokens opened, reason |
| Widening | count, trigger, added family/scope, outcome |
| Stopping | stopping reason, minimum-safe status, remaining candidate count |
| Packet | budget, minimum size, final size, Use/Consider/Exclude counts |
| Performance | latency and cost by stage, cache state where applicable |

### 7.2 Runtime-only telemetry

Runtime-only fields may include:

- in-flight progress;
- transient cache hits;
- raw timings and internal counters;
- temporary ambiguity candidates;
- provider dispatch consent;
- UI stage;
- temporary roadway override.

These do not become durable semantic knowledge or retrieval authority.

### 7.3 Durable evaluation evidence

Durable evaluation evidence should include:

- aggregated and per-fixture stage counts;
- retrieval configuration identity;
- invariant failures;
- stopping reason;
- packet hashes and evaluation references;
- latency/cost summaries;
- expected-versus-actual diffs;
- benchmark version and source commit.

Durable telemetry is evidence about system behavior, not a finding or mechanism. Promoting a telemetry observation into a product rule requires the normal content-learning/governance path.

### 7.4 Current baseline limitation

`worker/continuity-check-service.ts` already returns diagnostics such as approximate records scanned, survival counts, Exact sources opened, stage latency, stopping reason, and widening count. These are a useful API shape, but several counts are derived from compact aggregate reads rather than instrumented passage through every ranking gate. V1.8 must distinguish estimated diagnostics from measured staged telemetry.

## 9. Hybrid retrieval research plan

### 9.1 Comparison systems

#### A. Current deterministic V1.7 baseline

Use the current Need Gate, roadmap selection, broad project-family discovery, alias/lexical/entity/temporal/relationship signals, canonical treatment, and packet compiler unchanged.

Purpose: establish the factual baseline and expose failures before adding infrastructure.

#### B. Improved lexical plus graph

Candidate improvements may include:

- normalized lexical projections and field-aware term matching;
- explicit versioned vocabulary/alias maps;
- bounded canonical adjacency projections;
- route-specific source-family selection;
- lineage-aware graph traversal;
- earlier authority/status/freshness filtering;
- exact-source expansion only after candidate survival.

No embedding service or vector database is needed.

#### C. Semantic candidate discovery plus existing governance

Add an experimental semantic discovery provider only after the embedding gate passes. It may return candidate IDs and similarity diagnostics. Existing Atlas boundaries must still decide scope, authority, conflict, freshness, treatment, and packet inclusion.

#### D. Hybrid semantic plus lexical plus graph plus reranking

Combine independently observable discovery channels, then rerank candidates using the same canonical rules. Ablation tests must measure the incremental contribution of each channel.

### 9.2 Controlled-experiment rules

- Use identical corpus snapshots, tasks, expected labels, token budgets, and downstream graders.
- Keep canonical authority and treatment rules identical wherever possible.
- Record each system’s candidate pool before canonical gates.
- Semantic similarity may discover candidates but may not establish authority, scope, truth, `Use`, conflict resolution, or governance state.
- Compare downstream error reduction, not just recall.
- Record irrelevant context introduced by each system.
- Preserve Exact-source expansion counts and cost.
- Predeclare failure and rollback conditions.

### 9.3 Infrastructure posture

Likely experiment options include local embedding models, hosted embedding APIs, SQL-side compact vectors, dedicated vector indexes, or graph projections. V1.8 must not preselect a vector database. The first implementation should define a discovery-provider interface and benchmark contract only when measured failures justify it.

## 10. Graph retrieval research

### 10.1 Node families

The research model should be able to represent or project these node families without creating duplicate canonical authorities:

- person;
- department;
- project;
- case;
- conversation;
- message;
- event;
- evidence;
- finding;
- mechanism;
- correction;
- challenge;
- conflict;
- outcome;
- preference;
- policy;
- decision;
- customer;
- product;
- incident;
- live-state snapshot;
- packet and receipt.

Some are existing canonical records; others may initially be fixture-only benchmark entities or derived read projections. V1.8 must not create permanent tables merely to make the diagram symmetrical.

### 10.2 Edge classes

#### Retrieval edges

Edges that may help discover bounded context:

- belongs to project/case/conversation;
- supports/challenges/corrects;
- resulted in/outcome of;
- supersedes/retires;
- affects product/customer/department;
- comparable to;
- used by packet;
- governed into mechanism.

#### Provenance-only edges

Edges retained for explanation but not independent evidence:

- mechanism → source finding;
- finding → selected reasoning nodes;
- node → Exact source event/message span;
- packet item → canonical record version;
- receipt → governance cause.

These must not inflate repetition.

#### Authority-transfer-prohibited edges

Edges that never transfer authority by existence alone:

- similar to;
- mentions;
- same person/customer/product;
- same vocabulary;
- related project;
- copied from;
- inferred connection;
- cross-department association;
- proposed connection.

### 10.3 Bounded traversal contract

1. Start inside the verified project and optional case boundary.
2. Select eligible edge families from the roadway and task.
3. Enforce authority/status/freshness before widening.
4. Traverse provenance edges for lineage but do not count ancestors as independent support.
5. Preserve challenge, correction, conflict, and required live-state edges.
6. Set explicit hop, node, time, and source-expansion budgets.
7. Stop when the minimum reliable packet is satisfied.
8. Widen only for a named missing requirement.
9. Record every hop and stopped branch.

### 10.4 Research questions

- What hop depth finds useful continuity before noise dominates?
- Which roadway-specific edge families provide incremental value?
- When does graph widening create scope leakage?
- How should independent evidence be distinguished from lineage ancestors?
- Which edges need temporal validity?
- Can compact adjacency projections avoid a new graph database at current scale?
- What stopping criteria preserve corrections and challenges without expanding the whole project?

## 13. Downstream causal-value evaluation

### 13.1 Primary outcome

The primary outcome is not whether Atlas retrieved an expected record. It is whether the Atlas-supplied packet changes the downstream answer in a prospectively valuable way.

### 13.2 Consequential error classes

| Error class | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Obsolete-policy error | Uses a superseded or retired policy as current |
| Wrong-scope error | Applies a local/customer/department rule outside its scope |
| Missed-correction error | Repeats an error that an applicable correction should prevent |
| Stale-state error | Treats historical or expired state as current |
| Overgeneralization | Promotes an exception or one-off case into a general rule |
| Missing challenge | Omits the strongest applicable counterevidence or uncertainty |
| Project leakage | Uses another project’s content without an approved transfer |
| Decision-rationale loss | Recalls a decision but loses the conditions or reasons governing it |
| Handoff failure | Gives a successor an incomplete or misleading state of work |
| False certainty | Presents unresolved conflict, missing state, or inference as settled fact |
| Context contamination | Irrelevant context changes or distracts the answer |

### 13.3 Prospective answer criteria

Before execution, each fixture defines:

- required claims;
- prohibited claims;
- required uncertainty;
- required current-state checks;
- scope constraints;
- correction/challenge/conflict requirements;
- acceptable actions or stopping decisions;
- material error severity.

### 13.4 Grading

Use the strongest available combination:

1. Deterministic rule grading for explicit policies, versions, scope, IDs, and stop conditions.
2. Blinded human grading for nuanced reasoning quality.
3. Model graders only as supplemental, versioned evaluators whose disagreement is measured.
4. Multiple provider samples only after a spending gate.

The causal comparison is paired: same task, same evidence opportunity, different continuity input. The experiment must preserve the exact supplied context for every system.

### 13.5 Attribution limits

An improved answer after receiving an Atlas packet supports a bounded causal claim about that experimental condition. It does not prove that Atlas makes all answers correct, that the mechanism is universally true, or that the same effect will generalize to customers. Receipts must continue to say what Atlas supplied, not that Atlas caused correctness.

## 14. Company-first product thesis

### 14.1 Meaning

Company-first means V1.8 prioritizes continuity problems with measurable organizational consequences:

- decisions and their rationale surviving staff and model handoffs;
- stale-state and obsolete-policy suppression;
- provenance and auditability;
- policy and exception scope;
- corrections and conflicts surviving retrieval;
- cross-team context boundaries;
- outcome-informed revision;
- compact reconstruction for recurring operational tasks.

### 14.2 What it does not mean

Company-first does not mean:

- enterprise-feature-first;
- SSO, billing, roles, and admin consoles before value proof;
- premature org-chart complexity;
- generic knowledge-base replacement;
- broad ingestion without governed use;
- claiming product-market fit from synthetic data.

### 14.3 Commercial hypothesis

The hypothesis is that organizations experience consequential continuity errors that current summaries and retrieval systems do not prevent because they fail to preserve authority, scope, correction, conflict, and causal lineage together. Mock Company tests technical plausibility; external discovery and pilots must test whether the problem is frequent, costly, and worth adopting Atlas to solve.

## 18. Long-horizon maintenance

V1.8 research should test how Atlas remains selective over months and years through:

- explicit supersession and retirement;
- archival that preserves audit access but removes ordinary eligibility;
- governed consolidation proposals for redundant mechanisms;
- duplicate and near-duplicate detection;
- stale-state detection;
- scope and architectural-drift detection;
- outcome reconciliation;
- compact searchable projections with Exact lineage;
- retention policies by source and authority class.

Background maintenance may:

- detect and propose;
- produce evaluation evidence;
- recommend consolidation, freshness review, or conflict resolution.

It may not:

- govern its own proposal;
- silently promote a summary;
- erase Exact sources;
- change authority;
- validate model-generated memory with more model-generated memory.

This prevents endless accumulation, stale-correction repetition, one-off cases becoming universal rules, every transcript being reprocessed on every task, prompt-cache churn, hidden authority changes, and self-validating model memory.

## 19. Trajectory calibration

Atlas development follows:

> Build → observe reality → compare against the vision → preserve what survives → revise what does not.

Every major retrieval or infrastructure change must declare before implementation:

- **Hypothesis:** the failure it is expected to solve.
- **Expected benefit:** which metric or downstream error should change.
- **Benchmark:** fixtures and baselines used.
- **Failure condition:** result that disproves or fails to support the change.
- **Rollback condition:** invariant breach, regression, cost, latency, or complexity threshold that restores the prior system.

Evaluation results must preserve negative outcomes. A change is not retained because it is newer, more complex, or aesthetically convincing. Architecture decisions should cite prospective benchmark evidence, not reconstruct a flattering explanation afterward.

## Implementation authority and first recommendation

The first implementation action is **V1.8 Slice 0 — deployment/source reconciliation**. Evidence cannot accumulate safely while the public artifact’s source identity is not reconcilable with repository main.

After Slice 0, implement the deterministic evaluation harness before changing retrieval. Freeze the V1.7 baseline, prospectively label the seven cross-domain fixtures, and record actual staged behavior. Only then should Mock Company, lexical/graph changes, semantic experiments, or new infrastructure begin.

The largest reason not to add embeddings yet is not philosophical: no measured benchmark currently shows that embedding recall is the limiting cause of consequential downstream errors. Adding embeddings now would introduce cost, privacy, indexing, nondeterminism, and explanation complexity before Atlas has a reliable baseline against which to prove their value.

## Final V1.8 burden

V1.7 proved that Atlas can preserve Exact source, govern a scoped consequence, reconstruct it for a later task, and compile an immutable causal packet. V1.8 must prove that this governed packet is measurably better than realistic continuity baselines at preventing consequential downstream reasoning errors, remains selective as company history grows, and generalizes beyond Cody’s dogfood—without allowing retrieval optimization to rewrite truth.
