Post-mortem
The innings projection was almost exactly correct: Sugano pitched the projected 5⅓ innings. The principal error was estimating how many strikeouts he could generate during that workload. He faced 23 batters and reached five strikeouts through volume and effectiveness.
The major lessons were:
The workload contradiction was discovered but did not alter the final decision enough.
Once Sugano’s previous 6⅓-inning outing became known, the limited-workload thesis should have been substantially weakened, not merely accompanied by a small probability reduction.
Milwaukee’s contact profile and Sugano’s season-long strikeout average were overweighted.
Sugano’s current health, recent form, pitch leash and ability to outperform his broader baseline were underweighted.
Narrower does not automatically mean safer. The strikeout prop isolated one mechanism, but that mechanism depended heavily on an assumption already contradicted by current evidence.
The Brewers −1.5 audit identified the one-run failure cluster, but its counter-script remained too narrow. Colorado winning outright—particularly by multiple runs—deserved deeper reconstruction.
“Starter advantage” was treated too confidently. The actual game reversed the anticipated pitching script: Drohan allowed five runs while Sugano allowed two.
A late contradiction affecting a wager’s central mechanism should trigger a genuine rerank or pass decision, not merely a cosmetic confidence reduction.
Once the strikeout-under thesis weakened, Atlas should have reranked the alternatives. Under 8.5 may have become the more resilient thesis.
The loss should increase the future weight assigned to recent workload evidence, current health and leash, outright-underdog scripts, and late-discovered contradictions.
